# Cross-Room Impacts (Room-Myopia Categories)

Categories of items the carrier's adjuster commonly misses because they scope each room in isolation.

**This list is a starting point, not exhaustive.** Apply additional industry-standard cross-room considerations as the project warrants.

## Line-of-sight refinishing

Hardwood floors don't stop at a doorway. If you refinish the kitchen, the visible adjacent foyer or dining room must be refinished too — otherwise sheen, color, and grain finish won't match. Line of sight does not require shared walls — it requires that someone standing in Room A can see the floor of Room B. Hallways, foyers, and open-concept spaces extend the line of sight across the whole sightline corridor.

## Continuous-run material

Baseboard, crown molding, chair rail, wainscoting, trim — if these run through multiple rooms in one continuous installation, the supplement must include the full run, not just the damaged segment.

## Wall paint

If an interior wall is affected on the damaged-room side, the opposite face (in the adjacent room) often must be repainted to corner out properly. Same for a wall painted floor-to-ceiling around an opening.

## Ceiling continuity

Same logic on a continuous ceiling plane through multiple rooms.

## Transit-path protection

Heavy appliances, materials, debris all have to traverse undamaged rooms to reach the work zone. Floor protection (paper, hardboard, ram board), wall corner protection, dust barriers in the transit path — all required.

## Stairs as transit

If work is on a different floor, stairs become a transit path needing protection.

## Shared wall demolition

When demoing one side of a wall, the adjacent room is impacted (texture cracks, paint cracks, fixture vibration).

## HVAC system contamination

Dust generated in Room A travels through the HVAC system to every other room. Dust barriers at returns and post-construction duct cleaning are continuity items. Whole-house duct cleaning is sometimes appropriate even when only one room had damage, depending on HVAC zoning and the level of dust generated.

## Exterior finish matching

Across continuous wall planes, fences, fascia runs.
