# Roofing & Exterior Storm Damage Checklist

Use this reference for wind, hail, and storm-driven exterior claims. Adapted from Forensic Claim Analysis Checklists §5.

**This checklist is a starting point, not exhaustive.** Apply any additional reliable, independently-verifiable, industry-standard guidance appropriate to this specific roof, this specific peril, and this specific jurisdiction. Use `WebSearch` to verify code-driven items, manufacturer specs, and current IICRC / wind-mitigation standards before citing them.

## Why this matters

Field adjusters routinely under-scope roofs by focusing on the visible surface layer. Once the surface comes off, the underlying system is exposed — and the carrier's macro almost never includes the components that actually make a finished roof watertight, code-compliant, and warrantable.

## Items to audit for

### Tear-off and decking

- **Multiple legacy roofing layers**: tear-off cost rises non-linearly with each extra layer. If the contractor finds two or three layers underneath, supplement for the additional tear-off labor and tonnage.
- **Rotted decking**: discovered after tear-off. Supplement with structural carpentry line items (sheathing replacement, sister joist if needed) and the additional debris hauling.
- **Compromised trusses or framing**: requires a structural engineer's letter and separate framing line items.
- **Inadequate insulation** exposed during decking work — supplement to current code R-value (cross-references the Code/Ordinance Audit).

### Components the surface macro typically misses

- Starter shingles
- Ridge cap shingles (separate from field shingles)
- Hip and ridge shingles
- Drip edge (eave + rake; modern code typically requires both)
- Valley flashing (closed-cut vs. open valley spec)
- Pipe boots / plumbing jacks
- Step flashing at wall intersections
- Counter-flashing at chimneys
- Ridge vent or off-ridge vent
- Ice & water shield (eaves, valleys, around penetrations — code-driven, varies by climate zone)
- Underlayment (synthetic vs. felt — confirm carrier's spec)

### Wind-mitigation upgrades

If the home is in a high-wind jurisdiction (FL, coastal SE, parts of TX/OK), code may require:

- 6-nail nailing pattern
- Sealed-deck installation (e.g., peel-and-stick over entire deck)
- High-wind-rated shingles
- Specific hurricane clip / strap counts at trusses

These are code-driven — defer the actual code citation to the Code/Ordinance Audit, but flag the requirement here.

### Premium / specialty materials

- Class 4 impact-resistant shingles (CeDUR, IKO Nordic, Owens Corning Duration Storm, etc.) — supplement with manufacturer documentation; some carriers offer premium discount for this material.
- Synthetic slate, metal, tile — match-quality requirements may force full slope or full roof replacement.

### Material matching limitations

If the existing roof material is discontinued, incompatible with modern alternatives, or partial repair would produce a non-uniform appearance, the supplement must demand full replacement of the affected slope (and possibly the whole roof) based on the policy's matching/uniform-appearance language. Cite the specific policy clause if available.

### Exterior beyond the roof

- Gutters and downspouts (dent/crease pattern from hail, separation from soffit, drainage capacity)
- Fascia and soffit (impact damage, water staining)
- Chimney crown, masonry grout, cricket flashing
- Skylight curb, glazing, flashing kit
- Roof-mounted solar — detach and reset, plus electrician for re-commissioning. Get the original installer's invoice.
- Antennas, satellite dishes, weather vanes — R&R

### Hail-specific testing

- 10-foot × 10-foot test squares: contractor counts hail strikes per square. Standard for an approved hail claim is generally 8+ functional strikes per test square (verify carrier-specific threshold).
- Slope-by-slope assessment — carriers commonly approve only the impacted slopes, but the matching clause may force whole-roof replacement.
- Spatter / oxidation patterns on metal flashings, vents, gutters as forensic evidence of hail size.

## Common adjuster mistakes to look for

1. Approved field shingles but no starter, ridge cap, drip edge, or valleys.
2. One slope approved when matching language forces full-roof replacement.
3. Tear-off priced for one layer when photos clearly show multiple.
4. No allowance for high-wind nailing or ice & water shield where local code requires it.
5. Solar panels listed with zero detach/reset cost.
6. Gutter run-feet under-measured (often a third-party report fixes this).

## Supporting evidence to require

- EagleView, HOVER, or RoofR measurement report (eliminates dimensional disputes).
- Hyper-detailed annotated photos of each slope, each penetration, each test square.
- If specialty materials: manufacturer cut sheet + installer invoice.
- If structural decking damage: a separate "discovery" set of photos taken after tear-off.
