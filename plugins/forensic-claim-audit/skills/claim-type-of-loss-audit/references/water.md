# Water Mitigation & Flood Damage Checklist

Use this reference for water-loss claims. Adapted from Forensic Claim Analysis Checklists §6. The governing industry standards are **IICRC S500** (Water Damage Restoration) and **IICRC S520** (Mold Remediation). Any supplement that does not align with these standards will be dismissed.

**This checklist is a starting point, not exhaustive.** Apply any additional reliable, independently-verifiable, industry-standard guidance for the specific water category (Cat 1/2/3), class (1–4), and project conditions. Use `WebSearch` to verify the current IICRC equipment formulas, antimicrobial requirements, and code-required PRV testing before citing them.

## Why this matters

Water claims are the most aggressively scrutinized loss type because the cost escalates fast (drying time, mold, structural decay) and because the science is well-codified. The supplement succeeds or fails on whether you can prove scientific necessity for every line item.

## Required documentation (without which the supplement is incomplete)

- **Daily atmospheric drying log**: date, time, location, temperature, relative humidity, specific humidity (GPP), for each affected area, every day equipment was on site.
- **Moisture mapping**: structural moisture content readings of drywall, framing, subfloor, baseboard backing — initial, daily progression, dry standard.
- **Equipment count justification**: cubic-footage of each affected room mapped against the IICRC formula to scientifically justify the air mover and dehumidifier counts.
- **Categorization** of the water (Category 1 / 2 / 3 per S500) and the **Class** (1–4) — both drive labor codes.
- **Photos** of standing water (before extraction), saturated materials, equipment placement, demo cuts (flood cuts, baseboard cuts, ceiling cuts).

If any of these are missing, the contractor needs to gather them — flag the gap to the user.

## Items to audit for

### Extraction

- `WTREXT` is the base water-extraction code and only covers basic extraction. Higher-paying codes are often correct:
  - Hard surface extraction (different code)
  - Weighted subsurface extraction (carpet/pad)
  - Truck-mount extraction for high volume
  - After-hours / emergency labor rate (S500 emergency response window)
- Volume captured (gallons or square footage extracted) drives debris and disposal.

### Equipment

- **Air movers**: count justified by S500 formula (cubic footage × class adjustment ÷ ~50 CFM per mover for Class 2). Verify the formula against current IICRC.
- **Commercial dehumidifiers**: LGR (low-grain refrigerant) vs. desiccant — desiccant required for cold-weather or specific-humidity targets carriers often miss.
- **HEPA air scrubbers**: required if Cat 2/3 or any S520 mold concern.
- **Containment** (poly chambers, zipper doors) for Cat 3 or mold work.
- **Heaters / dehumidifiers in series** for stubborn structural drying.

Each piece of equipment has hours/days. Match equipment days to the drying log.

### Demolition (flood cuts, baseboard cuts, ceiling cuts)

- Drywall removal (full sheet, half sheet, 2-foot flood cut, 4-foot flood cut) — each is a different code.
- Baseboard removal & disposal (followed in Stage 4 by reinstall + paint).
- Insulation removal in wet wall cavities — a separate code from drywall.
- Subfloor removal where saturation has compromised structural integrity.
- Ceiling removal where water came from above.
- Cabinet detach to access wet substrate.

### Hidden saturation and wall-cavity drying

Water wicks. The supplement should account for:

- Wall-cavity drying systems (Injectidry, drying mats, negative-pressure systems)
- Sub-floor drying with interlocking mats
- Drilling weep holes into baseboard cavities
- Removing baseboard to allow wall-base drying

### Antimicrobial / sanitization

- Cat 2 water requires antimicrobial application; Cat 3 requires biocide and full PPE protocol.
- S520 mold remediation: containment, HEPA vacuum, antimicrobial, post-remediation verification (PRV) testing.

### Content manipulation

- Pack-out and storage (transitions to the Storage / Debris Audit in Stage 9 — flag here, account there).
- Pad-and-block (lifting furniture off wet floors so the floor can dry).

## Common adjuster mistakes

1. Approving 3 air movers when the cube/CFM math justifies 8.
2. Dehumidifier listed but no daily count — a carrier may pay for 1 day when 5 are documented.
3. Demolition listed as drywall removal but no insulation removal alongside.
4. Antimicrobial omitted on Cat 2/3 jobs.
5. No HEPA scrubber on a job with mold concern.
6. Drying log absent — fatal gap.

## Supporting evidence to require

- Daily drying logs (per above)
- Thermo-hygrometer readings
- Moisture meter readings (pin and pinless, with location notes)
- Photos of wall cavity moisture, weep holes, demo cuts
- Equipment placement diagram
- IICRC certifications of the techs on site (often referenced when carrier challenges scope)
